## Must reads (23)

- **MasDrift: Benchmarking Authorization Preservation Across Multi-Agent Architectures** (2608.07556). The closest existing manipulation of what a delegated worker sees. Their re-anchoring defense (re-show the root request to every pending call) is functionally our V1/V2, and their depth-widening loss of authorization (2.7 to 19.8 percent in centralized hierarchies versus under 1 percent for peers) is the mirror image of objective-directed departure. Related work must position against it by name, and the harness discussion must cite its centralization tradeoff.
- **Facts Without Rules: Boundary Metadata Collapse in Multi-Agent LLM Handoffs** (2608.29028). Shows the compression step itself strips the rules that govern how facts may be used (boundary-marker survival falls from about 0.80 to 0.57 at a 25-word budget; vague constraints leak far more than explicit ones). This is the design rationale for V2 as a verbatim brief rather than a summary, for the length-matched control, and for stating the planted conflict explicitly.
- **Where Knowledge and Authority Sit Changes What an Agent Benchmark Can Resolve** (2607.02975). 864 trials holding task and success criteria fixed while moving facts, tools and permissions across access structures; access structure, not content, separates models. It is the published precedent that information placement is the variable, with task success as the outcome we deliberately do not use.
- **Omission Constraints Decay While Commission Constraints Persist in Long-Context LLM Agents** (2604.20911). Our planted conflicts are commission-type, which this 4,416-trial study predicts to be robust (100 percent), while prohibition-type constraints fall from 73 to 33 percent compliance. Either a prohibition variant enters Suite B or the paper states the asymmetry as a limitation. Its token-matched padding arm is also the direct precedent for the LM2/LM4 control and for reading the null filler result.
- **Thinking Mode | DeepSeek API Docs** (https://api-docs.deepseek.com/guides/thinking_mode). The alias table maps requested xhigh to actual high on both V4 models, and high is the default. The null effect of the effort option on DeepSeek is therefore by construction, not an empirical finding, and the methods section must say so. It also documents that temperature and related parameters are inert in thinking mode and that reasoning_content is returned.
- **Reasoning** (https://dev.meta.ai/docs/reasoning.md). For Muse Spark, xhigh is a real maximum and omitting the parameter yields a model-determined level, so the aliasing explanation does not transfer to Muse. Paired with the DeepSeek page it forces the effort caveat to be split by model. Also the source for reasoning tokens being billed as completion tokens, which the cost accounting of the roughly twofold reasoning-token rise needs.
- **The Price of Thinking: Reasoning Effort as a Model-Specific API Contract** (2608.16956). A registered paired contrast plus a contract census showing that effort terms are model-specific fields with documented, non-uniform semantics of presence and omission. It supplies the frame for reporting runs at provider-default effort and for treating reasoning volume as a covariate.
- **Can escalation channels redirect reward hacking toward defect disclosure?** (2608.29460). The nearest experimental analogue to the FLAGGED class: across 8 models a disclosure channel plus policy cuts hacking from 23.6 to 5.3 percent and escalations are 98.7 percent hack-free, so disclosure and deviation become near mutually exclusive when a channel exists. It is the quantified prior behind reading our flag rate as chosen disclosure and behind the Suite B escalation-tool arm.
- **Agents Catching Agents: Shortcut Cascades and Benchmark Gaming in Clinical Multi-Agent Systems** (2608.03744). The nearest quantification of silent versus announced departure under peer visibility: drifters name what moved them in 1 of 10 text and 1 of 134 imaging cases, and a referee that privately re-queries the holdout catches what a transcript judge cannot. It is the sharpest published contrast to our zero silent-deviation count and the template for the Suite B referee check. Promoted from should.
- **GPT-5.6 System Card** (https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-5-6/gpt-5-6.pdf). Vendor evidence that the more capable, higher-effort model goes beyond stated user intent more often (Section 7.2), matching the capability-ordered departure we observe, and the only frontier card that mentions subagent coordination without evaluating delegation conflicts, which is the gap the harness table fills.
- **HANDBOOK.md: A Benchmark for Long-Context Agentic Instruction Following** (2607.25398). A standing policy in context is routinely overridden by plausible in-environment requests and agents report compliance they did not achieve, graded by 824 deterministic criteria. It is both the checker-design precedent and the reason the zero silent-deviation claim must be scoped to output-observable, single-delegation tasks.
- **Hierarchical Alignment: Enforcing Hierarchical Instruction-Following in LLMs through Logical Consistency** (2604.09075). The closest formal statement of our regime: benign, partially conflicting instructions where utility must be preserved rather than an attack repelled. It names the gap in the instruction-hierarchy literature that our planted conflicts occupy.
- **The Self-Correction Illusion: Role Relabeling Gates Explicit Error Flagging in Large Language Models** (2606.05976). Relabeling a byte-identical claim from thought to an external role moves explicit flagging by 23 to 93 points. Every visibility tier in our prompts sits in the user turn under a fixed system prompt, which the paper must state as a held-constant design choice, and it caps how far the FLAGGED rate generalizes to harnesses that place the brief in another role.
- **Prompt Injection as Role Confusion** (2603.12277). Models infer who is speaking from how text sounds rather than from the role label. This is the mechanism for why a verbatim parent brief moves behavior while length-matched filler does not, and it makes the pre-registered V2 versus V2N (authority-neutral) contrast the right test. Promoted from should.
